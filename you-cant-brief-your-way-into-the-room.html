You can't brief your way into the room

August 2026 · 3 min read

Tech BBQ. Back to back VC meetings, one room after another. I had prepared for weeks. The thing that decided the best meeting of the week was in none of my notes.

Start with what preparation buys you, because it is the price of entry. You never walk into a VC meeting exposed. You know your product cold, every claim you make and every number behind it. You know your pitch well enough to give it in three different lengths depending on how the conversation moves. You have read the fund, the partner, what they back and what they walk away from. Showing up without that reads as disrespect for their time, and they feel it in the first two minutes.

And you adapt live. The pitch you rehearsed is a starting position, not a script. If the partner leans into the technical risk, you go there. If they only care about the market, you drop the architecture. Reading which one it is, in the moment, is half the job, and you can only do it when the material is so internalized that you are not reciting it.

There is a shape to the minute that matters most. The idea in sixty seconds, clear enough that a stranger could repeat it back. Who it is for, and the why behind it, the reason it has to exist now. The traction, whatever it honestly is, stated plainly. Why they, specifically, should care. And who you are, the team, last. A partner buys into the problem and the pull before they fall for the founders. Open with the team and you sound like every other deck.

All of that can be learned from a book. What comes next cannot.

My best meeting of the week did not open with the pitch. It opened with an anecdote. The partner had just bought a house a few minutes from the town where I used to live, in the south of France. We spent the first stretch on that, two people who happened to know the same roads. By the time we reached what I was building, we were not a founder and an investor running a script. We were two people already enjoying the conversation. Everything after that landed differently.

The deck gets you into the room. What happens in the room is human.

That is the lesson no brief hands you. A VC meeting is a relationship you start, not a document you defend. And it runs on two things you cannot fake: energy and confidence. Energy, because a partner is betting on someone who will still be standing after three brutal years, and a flat room reads as a preview of that. Confidence, the quiet kind, because it tells them you believe the thing you are asking them to believe.

You cannot brief your way to either of those. No pitch coach installs them the night before. They come from having done the reps, from knowing your numbers so well you stop thinking about them and start reading the person across the table. Preparation is what frees you to be present. That is the part nobody says out loud. You do the homework so you can forget it and be fully in the room. The founder still reciting the deck has already lost it.

The rooms taught me more in a week than any prep material ever did. The prep was not the problem. The prep is the floor. The game is the human sitting across the table, and the only place to learn that game is the room itself.
