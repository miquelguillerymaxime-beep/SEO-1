Diary of a CCO · No. 03

The needy seller repels

September 2026 · 3 min read

Robert Greene has a law in The Laws of Human Nature that every salesperson learns the hard way. He calls it covetousness. We want what feels scarce, and what pulls away from us.

Push a deal and it backs up. The follow-ups pile on, the tone gets warmer, the discount comes out early, and the buyer feels all of it. Desperation has a smell, and it closes doors.

The needy seller repels. The seller who can walk away draws the room.

Here is the mechanism, because it is worth understanding and not just repeating. A buyer reads your certainty before they read your pitch. If you seem unsure they should buy, they inherit the doubt. Neediness is a signal, and the signal it sends is "I need this more than you do," which is the last thing a buyer wants to hear from the person asking for their money. People lean toward what others seem ready to walk away from. They pull back from what is pushed at them.

It has tells, and buyers catch every one. The third follow-up in a week. The discount offered before anyone asked. The tone that climbs from professional to eager to please. The call rescheduled three times to suit them. Each tell says the same sentence underneath: your yes matters more to me than my own product should.

There is a fake version of this and a real one, and buyers can tell them apart. Manufactured scarcity, the invented deadline, the "one spot left" that is not true, is manipulation, and people feel manipulation as fast as they feel desperation. Real scarcity is different. It comes from a full pipeline and from believing in what you sell. When you have other conversations and you are willing to lose this one, you do not perform detachment. You have it. And it changes the room.

The reframe that fixes it is to stop trying to be chosen and start trying to help them decide. A seller who needs the yes argues, oversells, and papers over bad fit. A seller who is ready to walk asks sharper questions, tells the truth about where the product falls short, and will say out loud "this is not for you." That honesty is magnetic, because it is the one thing a desperate seller can never afford to offer.

You cannot switch this on for a call. It is built before the call, in two things. Enough pipeline that no single deal owns you, which is the discipline I write about in Say no faster. And enough conviction that you would buy the thing yourself. Build those two, and the neediness leaves on its own, because there is nothing left feeding it.

The needy seller repels. The seller who can walk away draws the room. Same product, same price, opposite outcome. The only variable that moved was who needed the deal more.
